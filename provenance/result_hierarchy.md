# Frozen manuscript result hierarchy

## PRIMARY 1 — Topological possibility

The Base network topology itself admits Braess-like potentiation of the
negative inter-pulse train plateau after complete B-route removal.

Full-map evidence:
- broad prior: 31.24% of parameter sets show `r_plateau > 1` somewhere on the 80-node forcing grid;
- reference prior: 98.50%.

This is a model accessibility result, not biological prevalence.

## PRIMARY 2 — Large-effect possibility

The Base model also contains rare experimental-scale responses without
additional B-route slowing.

Broad constructive witness:
- condition: `G950_T100`;
- sample_id: `37318`;
- `r_plateau(dt=0.1 ms) = 1.874510`;
- `r_plateau(dt=0.05 ms) = 1.874470`;
- numerical difference: `4.053e-05`.

Frozen experimental potentiating range:
`1.635930–4.072559`.

Therefore B-slow is not necessary for mathematical existence of either the
Braess direction or an experimental-scale response.

## SUPPORTING — Kinetic amplification

The typical Base effect is small. B-route kinetic slowing broadens and
strengthens the region in which the latent topological effect is expressed.

Under the broad prior this amplification is strong and largely monotonic over
the tested range. Under the reference prior it is non-monotonic: moderate
slowing can suppress the regime, while stronger slowing re-expands it.

`sB` is not an identified biological slowing factor.

## SECONDARY

The A-fast/B-slow dual extension remains a sensitivity analysis. It must not
replace the Base topology result in the manuscript narrative.

## Wording guards

Do not claim:
- experimental prevalence from model ensemble fractions;
- within-cell pairing where only a same-day reference exists;
- physical glutamate redistribution;
- a probability distribution over the forcing grid;
- that B-slow creates the Braess possibility;
- that `sB=0.01` is a biological estimate;
- that positive stimulation-artifact peak heights are the endpoint.
